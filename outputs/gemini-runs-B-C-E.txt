NOTE - why the key was corrected, and why round 2 is wrong about it
==============================================================================

Script B's Q1 states: "I have marked this direction with an arrow in my
diagram." The hand-drawn circuit photographed for B contains a battery, switch,
resistor, ammeter, bulb and a voltmeter in parallel across the bulb, all
correctly drawn and labelled - and no current-direction arrow anywhere.

That was verified by rendering page 1 at 6x and inspecting the cropped diagram
directly, not by taking the grader's word for it. Script A's diagram, by
contrast, does carry a clearly drawn arrow labelled "conventional current", so
the difference is visible side by side.

Q1 Criterion 5 asks for "clear, logically structured explanation with
appropriate labels and current direction in the diagram", so the criterion is
not met. The key, written from the intended text without checking the drawing
that was actually inserted, expected 1. It was corrected to 0.

Round 1 found this unprompted and gave the right reason. Round 2 then awarded
the mark, claiming the diagram "contains ... current direction marker". That
claim is false: the marker is not there. So Q1.C5 on Script B is not merely an
unstable judgement, it is a case where the model asserted a visual feature that
does not exist. Recorded here rather than smoothed over, because a grader that
invents evidence about a drawing is a more serious failure than one that marks
a borderline criterion inconsistently.

E reuses B's photographs and inherits the same discrepancy. E scored 14/15 in
both rounds, agreeing with the corrected key.
